# Comparision between geometric and physics based deformation models - DH2323 Computer Graphics and Interaction Final project at KTH

## Abstract
This paper presents the implementation and comparative evaluation of two object
deformation methods, Free-Form Deformation (FFD) and Position-Based Dynamics (PBD),
within an immersive virtual reality environment. Both methods were implemented in Unity
and deployed on a Meta Quest 3 headset. A qualitative user study involving four participants
was conducted to assess the influence of each method on perceived visual realism, physical
plausibility and overall interaction quality. Participants favoured the geometry-based FFD ap-
proach in all areas. This makes it more suited to soft, compliant materials, and participants
found it more predictable and satisfying to interact with. Although the physics-based PBD
implementation is theoretically well-suited to soft-body simulation, it did not achieve the de-
sired level of responsiveness. This limited the comparability of the two conditions. This study
should be viewed as a pilot investigation that highlights methodological considerations for
future research rather than drawing generalisable conclusions. The findings suggest that FFD
may offer a more accessible route to achieving convincing soft-body interaction in mobile VR,
while also raising questions for more controlled follow-up studies
